Level 1. Read the poem two times. Then do the activities. Your answers save on this device.

1 Words and meanings

Choose the meaning of each word.

2 Put the chain in order

The poem is a chain. One thing becomes the next thing. Choose the correct order.

3 Grammar: become or becomes?

Many things (thoughts, words, actions, habits) → become. One thing (character, a habit, he, she) → becomes.

4 Grammar: one and many

Write the plural. One thought → two thoughts.

5 Grammar: Be careful of your ___ .

This is a command. We use it to tell someone to be careful. Write five commands from the poem. Use the word bank.

Level 2. Now you explain the poem in your own words, and use the language of cause and effect.

1 Say it another way

Rewrite each line in your own words. Do not copy the poem.

2 Cause and effect connectors

The poem only uses because. Rewrite the same idea with a different connector.

Your thoughts become your words, so choose them carefully.
Careless words lead to careless actions.
Repeating an action causes it to turn into a habit.
Her habits shaped her character; as a result, people trusted her.

3 Combine the sentences

Make one sentence from two. Use because, which, and then, or if.

4 Your own chain

Think of one real thought you had this year. Follow it through the chain. Be honest — a small chain is fine.

5 If … then

The poem is really a warning about the future. Write three warnings with if.

6 Short answer

Level 3. A famous quotation is not the same thing as a true statement. Your job here is to test it.

1 Who actually said this?

Search this quotation online. You will find it credited to Lao Tzu, the Buddha, Gandhi, Margaret Thatcher, and an American businessman named Frank Outlaw, whose 1977 version is the earliest one researchers have found in print. Nobody can show that Lao Tzu or Gandhi ever wrote it.

2 How much do you agree?

Choose a position, then defend it. A strong answer uses an example.

3 What does the chain leave out?

The poem says character becomes destiny. That means: what happens to you is caused by the kind of person you are. Test that claim against the real world.

4 How the poem is built

Each line picks up the last word of the line before it: thoughts → thoughts, words → words. The Greek name for this device is anadiplosis. It makes an argument feel like a machine — each step seems to prove the next one.

Now write your own five-line chain using the same device. Choose your own starting word (money, silence, fear, practice, kindness…).

5 Essay planner

Build an argument. You are not summarizing the poem — you are answering it.
