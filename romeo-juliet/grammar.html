Telling what happened

When you retell the story of a scene — what the characters did, in order — you use the simple past.

Most verbs: add -ed. refuse → refused  ·  climb → climbed  ·  arrive → arrived
Verbs ending in a consonant + y: change y to -ied. carry → carried  ·  marry → married
Short verbs, one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant. stab → stabbed  ·  plan → planned  ·  beg → begged

Retelling uses past. Analysis uses present.

Here is the whole rule in one line: events happened, but the text always says.

Retelling — what the characters did: Romeo killed Tybalt in the square.
Analysis — what the writer does and what the text shows: Shakespeare shows Romeo losing control. The scene reveals his grief.

Why? Because the play is not finished. Every time someone opens the book, Romeo kills Tybalt again. The story is always happening now.

Comparing two things to say something true

Simile uses like or as. She hangs upon the cheek of night like a rich jewel.
Metaphor says one thing is the other. Juliet is the sun.

Finding it is the easy part. The work is explaining it, and there are three steps:

1. Name the two things. Romeo compares Juliet to the sun.
2. Name what they share. Both give light and both are the center of everything around them.
3. Say why the writer chose it. Shakespeare uses this to show that Romeo already organizes his whole world around her.
