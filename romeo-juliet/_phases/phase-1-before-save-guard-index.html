Romeo and Juliet — ELD Unit

Scenes Grammar Assessment Teacher

Five weeks · Grades 9–10 · Newcomer through Bridging

Two families. One city. Everybody already knows how it ends.

Two households, both alike in dignity,
In fair Verona, where we lay our scene,
From ancient grudge break to new mutiny,
Where civil blood makes civil hands unclean.
From forth the fatal loins of these two foes
A pair of star-cross'd lovers take their life;
Whose misadventured piteous overthrows
Do with their death bury their parents' strife.

Shakespeare gives away the ending in the first fourteen lines. That is the gift for our students: nobody has to guess what happens. All the work goes into how and why.

The four student tools

Scene Reading GuidesEight scenes. Easier retelling and Shakespeare's original side by side, tap-to-translate glossary, questions at three levels, group roles, autosave. Grammar & Language LabPast tense for retelling, literary present for analysis, simile and metaphor. Auto-checked practice at three levels. Assessment CenterThree checkpoint quizzes, final test, scaffolded essay planner, performance rubric, portfolio reflection. Teacher DashboardLive progress, auto-scores, written responses, CSV export.

What this unit is trying to do

Essential question: When the people who love you and the people who hate you are the same people, what are you supposed to do?

Unit thesis students build toward: the tragedy is not caused by love, and not caused by fate alone — it is caused by adults who refuse to stop fighting, and by young people who move too fast in a world that gave them no slow options.

By the end, students can:

  • Retell the plot of a scene in accurate past tense, in sequence, in their own words.
  • Quote a line of Shakespeare and explain what it means in plain English.
  • Identify a simile or metaphor and explain what two things are being compared and why.
  • Write an analytical paragraph in the literary present with a claim, evidence, and explanation.
  • Perform a short scene with accurate pronunciation, volume, and an acting choice they can defend.

Day-by-day lesson plans

Every day is a 55-minute block. Open one to read it; hit Print to get all 25 on paper.

Week 1 · Building the world before the words
Day 1 — Whose side are you on?
Loyalty, family, and the cost of taking sides

Students can state an opinion about family loyalty and give one reason.

Do Now
On a half sheet: Someone insults your family. What do you do? Three choices — walk away / say something back / get others involved. Stand in a corner of the room matching your choice.
Mini-lesson
Frame: I would ______ because ______. Model with a personal story. Teach feud, grudge, loyalty, honor with gesture and picture.
Work time
Anticipation guide in the Assessment Center — twelve statements, Agree/Disagree, with the "Before" column only. Partners compare two answers.
Share
Three volunteers read a frame aloud. No debate yet — just hearing the range.
Exit
One sentence: the statement you feel strongest about, and why.
Day 2 — Verona, 1595: rules of the world
Background building: city, church, sword, father

Students can name three rules of Shakespeare's world that are different from theirs.

Do Now
Picture sort: ten images (sword, church, mask, letter, market, arranged marriage portrait...). Modern or old?
Mini-lesson
Four rules on the board: fathers choose husbands; men carry swords; the church controls marriage; a city prince can exile you. Students copy with a drawing next to each.
Work time
Map of Verona activity — label Capulet house, Montague house, the square, the church, the tomb. Distance matters later.
Share
Which rule would be hardest for you to live under?
Exit
In 1595, a father could ______.
Day 3 — Shakespeare and the shape of a tragedy
Who wrote this, and why it sounds strange

Students can explain that a tragedy tells you the ending at the start.

Do Now
Watch the trailer (Drive: Romeo and Juliet — Trailer.mp4). Write three things you saw.
Mini-lesson
Shakespeare in 90 seconds: England, 1595, plays for everybody, words invented. Then: tragedy = the main characters die and it was avoidable.
Work time
Read the Prologue three ways: teacher reads for rhythm, students echo, students read in pairs. Then the Prologue gloss on the Scenes page — what does star-cross'd mean?
Share
Whole class: what does Shakespeare tell us on page one? Why would a writer do that?
Exit
Prologue in one sentence, own words.
Day 4 — Character flash cards and the two houses
Eight people, one color system

Students can sort major characters by house and state one relationship.

Do Now
Prologue cloze from memory — six blanks.
Mini-lesson
Introduce the red/blue/gold system used in every tool. Model: Tybalt is Juliet's cousin. He is a Capulet.
Work time
Character card sort in teams. Then build a family tree on butcher paper with string between related characters.
Share
Gallery walk — find one mistake on another team's tree, sticky-note it kindly.
Exit
Three sentences: ______ is ______'s ______.
Day 5 — Group roles and how we read together
Summarizer, Visualizer, Connector, Predictor

Students can perform their reading-group role with a short practice text.

Do Now
Name three things a good reading group does.
Mini-lesson
Four role cards, one at a time, with a frame each: First… then… finally… / I picture… / This reminds me of… / I think next…
Work time
Practice round on a one-paragraph news story. Everyone tries all four roles for 90 seconds each.
Share
Assign permanent groups of four. Post them.
Exit
My role is ______. My job is to ______.
Week 2 · Act I — a fight, a father, a party
Day 6 — Act I Scene 1: biting your thumb
How a small insult becomes a street fight

Students can retell the brawl in order and identify who escalates it.

Do Now
What is the smallest thing you have seen start a big argument?
Mini-lesson
Teach insult, draw, quarrel, mutiny. Act out the thumb-biting with a student. Laughter is the point — then ask what it cost.
Work time
Scenes page, Act I Scene 1. Easier version first for everyone; Original excerpt for those ready. Group roles active. Questions Start + Build.
Share
Escalation ladder on the board: who made it worse at each rung?
Exit
The fight started because ______ .
Day 7 — Simple past: regular and irregular
Telling what happened

Students can retell five events from Act I Scene 1 in accurate simple past.

Do Now
Ten verbs on the board. Which past forms do you already know?
Mini-lesson
-ed rule and the twelve irregulars this play needs most: draw/drew, fight/fought, break/broke, speak/spoke, come/came, take/took, run/ran, say/said, see/saw, leave/left, meet/met, die/died.
Work time
Grammar Lab, Past Tense tab, Levels 1–3. Finish with the Act I retelling sort.
Share
Three students read their retelling aloud; class listens for one verb to fix.
Exit
Five-sentence retell, turned in on the page.
Day 8 — Act I Scene 3: Juliet is told to consider Paris
Thirteen years old and already being married off

Students can describe Juliet's position and quote one thing she says about obedience.

Do Now
How old were your parents when they married? Whose choice was it?
Mini-lesson
obey / disobey / consent. Juliet's careful answer: she will look to like — she agrees to try, not to obey. Model why that word choice matters.
Work time
Scenes page, Act I Scene 3 reading. Then a two-column chart: what Juliet says / what Juliet probably means.
Share
Is Juliet obedient? Vote, then defend.
Exit
Juliet is smart because ______ .
Day 9 — Act I Scene 5: the party and the shared sonnet
Fourteen lines, two speakers, one kiss

Students can explain the pilgrim/saint metaphor and why they build it together.

Do Now
Masks passed around. What can you do behind a mask that you can't do without one?
Mini-lesson
Read the sonnet as a duet — teacher is Romeo, class is Juliet, then swap. Then unpack: he calls her a shrine, himself a pilgrim. She plays along and wins the exchange.
Work time
Scenes page, Act I Scene 5. Stretch questions for the ready group. Others complete the Easier text + Build questions.
Share
Tybalt sees Romeo. Predict Act III now, write it down, seal it.
Exit
My only love sprung from my only hate — explain in your own words.
Day 10 — Quiz 1 and Act I storyboard
Checkpoint

Students demonstrate Act I comprehension, vocabulary, and past tense.

Do Now
Two-minute partner quiz-quiz-trade with the character cards.
Mini-lesson
Quiz directions and the honest reminder: this checks what we taught, not what you already knew.
Work time
Assessment Center, Quiz 1. Auto-scored. Early finishers begin the six-panel Act I storyboard.
Share
Review the three most-missed items together.
Exit
Storyboard panel 1 drawn and captioned in past tense.
Week 3 · Act II — the balcony and the plan
Day 11 — Act II Scene 2: Juliet is the sun
The most famous scene in English

Students can read the balcony passage aloud with meaning and gloss four lines.

Do Now
Who have you talked to at night when you were supposed to be asleep?
Mini-lesson
Kill the myth: wherefore means why, not where. She is not looking for him. She is asking the universe why he has to be a Montague.
Work time
Scenes page, Act II Scene 2. Read Original in pairs, one line each, three passes: for words, for meaning, for feeling.
Share
Two pairs perform four lines. Class gives one specific compliment.
Exit
What's in a name? — answer it yourself, in one sentence.
Day 12 — Simile and metaphor
Comparing two things to say something true

Students can identify simile vs. metaphor and explain the comparison.

Do Now
Finish: My phone is like ______ because ______.
Mini-lesson
Simile uses like or as; metaphor says it is. Then the three-step explanation: name the two things → name what they share → say why the writer chose it.
Work time
Grammar Lab, Simile & Metaphor tab. Then the quote hunt across Acts I–II in the Scenes page.
Share
Class ranks the four best comparisons found.
Exit
Write one simile and one metaphor about the feud.
Day 13 — Friar Lawrence and a plan made of hope
Wisely and slow; they stumble that run fast

Students can state the Friar's reason for agreeing and judge whether it is good.

Do Now
An adult helps you do something your parents said no to. Good adult or bad adult?
Mini-lesson
Herbs that heal and kill from the same plant — the Friar's own metaphor for people. Teach herb, remedy, poison, haste.
Work time
Scenes page, Act II Scenes 3 and 6. Then: list the Friar's plan in four steps and mark where it could fail.
Share
Whole-class verdict on the Friar. Record the tally — revisit on Day 23.
Exit
These violent delights have violent ends — predict what he means.
Day 14 — Acting workshop
Volume, pause, face, hands

Students can deliver four lines with a defended acting choice.

Do Now
Say "I'm fine" four ways: angry, sad, lying, actually fine.
Mini-lesson
The four acting tools poster. One line, three deliveries, class votes on which serves the scene.
Work time
Groups choose a passage from Acts I–II. Block it. Mark the script for pauses and stress.
Share
Two groups run it. Feedback frame: When you ______, I felt ______.
Exit
My acting choice is ______ because the character ______ .
Day 15 — Quiz 2 and mid-unit conference
Checkpoint

Students demonstrate Act II comprehension and figurative language skill.

Do Now
Quick-write: the best line in the play so far, and why.
Mini-lesson
Quiz directions. Reminder that the Stretch questions are optional and ungraded for level.
Work time
Assessment Center, Quiz 2. Teacher pulls four students for a two-minute conference each while others work.
Share
Return Quiz 1 data as a class picture, not individual scores.
Exit
One thing I want to get better at in the next two weeks.
Week 4 · Act III — the day everything breaks
Day 16 — Act III Scene 1: Mercutio and Tybalt
A plague o' both your houses

Students can sequence the deaths and explain Romeo's role in each.

Do Now
Have you ever tried to stop a fight and made it worse?
Mini-lesson
Slow-motion staging of the arm-grab: Romeo steps between them, and Tybalt reaches under his arm. Mercutio dies because Romeo tried to keep the peace.
Work time
Scenes page, Act III Scene 1. Cause-and-effect chain with six boxes.
Share
Whose fault is it? Four-corner debate: Romeo / Tybalt / Mercutio / the parents.
Exit
O, I am fortune's fool — does Romeo believe he had a choice?
Day 17 — The literary present
Retelling uses past. Analysis uses present.

Students can switch between past-tense retelling and present-tense analysis on purpose.

Do Now
Two sentences on the board, one right and one wrong. Which is which, and why?
Mini-lesson
The rule in one line: events happened, but the text always says. Romeo killed Tybalt / Shakespeare shows Romeo losing control.
Work time
Grammar Lab, Literary Present tab, all three levels. Then convert five of your own retelling sentences into analysis sentences.
Share
Chart the difference on the wall; leave it up through Week 5.
Exit
Two sentences about the same moment, one in each tense.
Day 18 — Act III Scene 5: Juliet refuses
The father who was kind on Monday

Students can describe the shift in Capulet and support it with a quote.

Do Now
What does an adult sound like right before they lose their temper?
Mini-lesson
Put Capulet's Act I line next to his Act III line. Same man, three days apart. Teach threaten, wretch, disobedient.
Work time
Scenes page, Act III Scene 5. Then: Juliet asks four people for help and gets nothing. List them and what each one does.
Share
The Nurse's betrayal — is it betrayal, or is she trying to save Juliet?
Exit
Juliet is alone because ______ .
Day 19 — Building the analytical paragraph
Claim, evidence, explanation

Students can write one paragraph with all three parts.

Do Now
Sort six strips into claim / evidence / explanation.
Mini-lesson
Model a full paragraph live on Capulet, thinking aloud. Then show a weak one and fix it together.
Work time
Assessment Center, Essay Planner. Level-matched frames. Everyone drafts one body paragraph.
Share
Partner check with the three-part checklist only — no line editing yet.
Exit
Paragraph saved on the page.
Day 20 — Quiz 3 and scene assignment
Checkpoint, then casting

Students demonstrate Act III understanding and receive performance scenes.

Do Now
Three-sentence summary of Act III from memory.
Mini-lesson
Performance expectations and the rubric, read together, line by line.
Work time
Assessment Center, Quiz 3. Then groups receive and read through their performance scene.
Share
Each group states its scene and one thing it will be hard to act.
Exit
My part is ______. My first line is ______ .
Week 5 · Acts IV–V, and everything due
Day 21 — Act IV: the potion
A plan with too many moving parts

Students can list the steps of the plan and name two ways it could fail.

Do Now
What is the riskiest plan you have ever agreed to?
Mini-lesson
Teach vial, distilled, tomb. Walk the timeline on the board hour by hour. The letter is the weak link — circle it in red.
Work time
Scenes page, Act IV. Then: Juliet drinks it alone. Write her three fears in her own voice.
Share
Where does this plan break? Predictions recorded.
Exit
The plan will fail because ______ .
Day 22 — Act V Scene 3: the tomb
Thirty seconds too early

Students can explain the timing of the ending and its effect on the reader.

Do Now
Silent reading of the last twelve lines. No talking for four minutes.
Mini-lesson
Count it out: she wakes moments after he drinks. Ask what Shakespeare is doing to us on purpose.
Work time
Scenes page, Act V Scene 3. Then the Prince's final speech — what changes in Verona, and is it enough?
Share
Return the Day 9 sealed predictions. Read a few.
Exit
Never was a story of more woe — do you agree? One sentence.
Day 23 — Final test and essay drafting
What we know, on paper

Students complete the summative test and a full essay draft.

Do Now
Revisit the Day 13 Friar verdict tally. Has anyone changed their mind?
Mini-lesson
Test format walk-through; reminder that the essay is graded on the rubric they already have.
Work time
Assessment Center: Final Test, then essay drafting in the planner.
Share
None — protect the work time.
Exit
Essay draft saved and marked complete.
Day 24 — Dress rehearsal and revision
One more pass on everything

Students revise an essay paragraph and rehearse with feedback.

Do Now
Read your own paragraph aloud quietly. Mark one sentence to fix.
Mini-lesson
Two revision moves only: add a because, and change one weak verb.
Work time
Half the period on revision, half on rehearsal. Teacher rotates with the rubric, giving one glow and one grow per group.
Share
Each group performs 30 seconds for one other group.
Exit
One change I am making before tomorrow.
Day 25 — Performances and portfolio
Curtain

Students perform their scene and complete the unit reflection.

Do Now

Mini-lesson
Audience contract: full attention, applause for everyone, feedback on the form only.
Work time
Performances, filmed. Peer feedback forms during transitions.
Share
Portfolio assembly and reflection in the Assessment Center.
Exit
The hardest thing about this unit was ______. The thing I am proudest of is ______ .

Differentiation built into the tools

  • Two texts, one page. Every scene has an Easier retelling in controlled English and Shakespeare's original. Students choose, or you set the default. Nobody is reading a different handout in a different corner.
  • Three question tiers. Start (literal, mostly select-and-check), Build (short constructed response with frames), Stretch (analytical, open). The tier is visible but not labeled as a level in front of students.
  • Six-language glossary. Tap any highlighted word for the definition plus Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, Vietnamese, Thai, and Nepali. Translations are built into the page, so they work offline and they print.
  • Group roles. Each scene has a role task for Summarizer, Visualizer, Connector, and Predictor, so a newcomer has a real job in a mixed group.
  • Print everything. Every page has a print button that strips the interface and gives you a clean worksheet, glossary included, for students who need paper or for a sub.
