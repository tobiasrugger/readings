Prewriting

Taking notes

Before you write, you need ideas. One way to collect ideas is to ask questions and write down the answers. When you take notes, you do not write full sentences. You write only the important words.

Organization

Paragraph form

A paragraph is a group of sentences about one topic — one idea, and only one. A paragraph also has a shape you can see from across the room.

Six rules for every paragraph in this class
  1. Write the title on the top line, in the center.
  2. Skip a line. Start writing on the third line.
  3. Leave margins of about one inch on the left and the right.
  4. Indent the first sentence about half an inch.
  5. Start each new sentence right after the one before it.
  6. A title is short, and it is usually not a full sentence.

Grammar & Mechanics

What is a sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that has a subject, has a verb, and expresses a complete thought. If a group of words is missing one of those three, it is a fragment — not a sentence.
Group of wordsProblem
Walked home in the rain.no subject — who walked?
The tall man near the door.no verb — what did he do?
Because the bus was late.not a complete thought — what happened?

One exception: in a command, the subject you is understood and not written. Sit down. and Help! are complete sentences.

Sentence or fragment?

Choose. If it is a fragment, a second question will appear — say what is missing.

Grammar & Mechanics

Subjects and verbs

The subject is who or what the sentence is about. The verb tells what the subject does or is.
A sentence can have more than one subject, or more than one verb, or both.

Find them

Tap a word once for subject, twice for verb, three times to clear it. Tap only the main word — skip the, my, a.

Grammar & Mechanics

Action verbs and linking verbs

An action verb tells what the subject does. He kicked the ball.
A linking verb does no action. It connects the subject to a word that describes or renames it. He is tired.
The test: replace the verb with is or are. If the sentence still makes sense, it is probably a linking verb. The soup tastes salty → The soup is salty. ✓ linking

Common linking verbs: be, seem, become, look, feel, taste, smell, sound.

Sentence Structure

Simple sentences

A simple sentence has one subject–verb combination. But the subject can be double, and the verb can be double, and it is still simple.
PatternExample
SVThe rain stopped.
SSVMy brother and my sister argue constantly.
SVVElena cooked dinner and washed the dishes.
SSVVTomás and Kevin ran and jumped in the yard.

Name the pattern

Sentence Structure

Connecting words: and, or

Use and to join two positive things. I like mangoes and papayas.
Use or to join two negative things. I don't like onions or peppers.
Use or to show a choice. We can take the bus or the train.
Three or more things in a list get commas: She bought rice, beans, and oil.

Sentence Structure

Sentence combining

Short sentences that repeat the same words sound tired. Joining them makes writing sound like an adult wrote it.

Rules: do not repeat a word you already used · use and or or · use commas in a list of three or more · the result must still be one correct sentence.

Watch one happen:

My uncle is tall. My uncle is quiet. My uncle is patient.

→ My uncle is tall, quiet, and patient.

The Writing Process

Freewriting

Freewriting means writing without stopping for a set time. You do not fix spelling. You do not erase. You do not stop to think. If you get stuck, write the last word again until a new idea comes. The goal is ideas, not a clean paper.
