Day-by-day plan
DAY 1 · 45 MIN

Hook and prediction

  • Warm-up (5): Show the two illustrations only. Ask: Which insect works? Which insect plays? How do you know? Collect words on the board — this pre-loads lazy, hardworking, save.
  • Anticipation guide (10): Students open Step 1 and answer the BEFORE column only. Read each statement aloud; students physically move to one side of the room for statement 4 ("Making music, art, or stories is real work"). Do not resolve the disagreement — you will come back to it on Day 4.
  • Read Parts 1–2 (20): Read aloud once at normal speed while students follow. Second read: stop at the glossed words and let students tap for their language. Newcomers switch to Easier; the numbered Standard level is the default.
  • Prediction (10): Question 6. Sentence frame is on the page. Two or three students share.

Exit One sentence: what will happen to the grasshopper in winter?

DAY 2 · 45 MIN

Finish the fable, then rebuild it

  • Re-entry (5): Three students read their predictions from yesterday.
  • Read Parts 3–4 (15): Take the dialogue in two voices — half the class is the grasshopper, half is the ant. The repetition of "You should have…" is the point; let them hear it four times before you ever name the grammar.
  • Questions 7–11 (10): Question 11 ("Was the ant right?") is the one worth arguing about. Some of your students have lived on the wrong side of that door.
  • Sequencing (10): The eight events. Students number them, press Check my order, fix, and then retell the whole fable to a partner using only the eight sentences.
  • Close (5): Question 12 — the moral in one sentence.

Print option Print the sequencing section and cut the eight sentences into strips for a physical sort before students do it on screen.

DAY 3 · 45 MIN

Grammar that came out of the story

  • Notice it (8): Whole class, on the board: "You should have made a house." Ask: Did he make a house? Is it too late? That is the whole lesson. Everyone gets this, at every level.
  • Split (25): Newcomers → Level 1 (simple past, regular and irregular). Developing → Level 2 (would / used to for repeated past). Ready → Level 3 (should have + past participle). Everyone finishes with the Bonus connectors, because they need them for Day 5.
  • Production (10): Each level ends with a short writing task. Level 3's advice task is the one to share out loud.
  • Close (2): Two students give the grasshopper advice using should have.

Why this grammar Should have + past participle is high-frequency, emotionally useful, and almost never taught to beginners. It is sitting in the last four lines of a fable they already understand.

DAY 4 · 45 MIN

The argument

  • Survey (8): Step 3, question 1. The meter gives each student a percentage. Poll the room: how many are over 50% ant? Write the split on the board.
  • Article 1 (12): Savers and spenders. Three levels on the same page. Comprehension a–c.
  • Article 2 (10): Tsui's counter-argument — the grasshopper as an artist doing invisible work. Read this one aloud to everybody, at every level. Then go back to anticipation statement 4 and ask if anyone has changed their mind.
  • Four corners (10): ANT / GRASSHOPPER / BOTH / IT DEPENDS. Students stand in a corner and give one reason. They may move once if someone convinces them.
  • T-chart (5): Students fill both columns before they leave, even if they already know their side.
DAY 5 · 45 MIN

Write it

  • Quote work (10): The quote bank, then the three steps — introduce, explain, connect. Model one on the board with a quote nobody picked.
  • Plan (10): Five boxes, five frames. Nobody starts the paragraph until all five boxes have something in them.
  • Draft (15): Word counter turns to a check mark at 60 words. Remind them: one quote, one should have.
  • Checklist + peer read (8): Students tick the seven boxes honestly, then read their paragraph out loud to one partner. Reading aloud catches more errors than re-reading silently.
  • Close (2): Back to Step 1 — fill in the AFTER column of the anticipation guide. Ask who moved, and why.
DAY 6 · 35 MIN

Assessment

  • Parts A–D auto-score (32 points): vocabulary, sequence, comprehension, grammar.
  • Part E is yours to score (8 points) with the rubric printed on the page.
  • Starred questions are extra credit and cost nothing to skip — they let your strongest students stretch without changing anyone else's test.
  • Submission locks. If a student needs a reopen, clear their turned_in flag in Supabase.
Differentiation and scaffolds

StandardThe numbered 20-sentence version — numbers let students cite ("In sentence 11…") and make sequencing easyDefault for the class
ChallengeLiterary retelling with subordinate clauses, an ambiguous ending, and the La Fontaine note that the ant is not generousLong-term ELs, reclassified students, anyone who finishes early

Answer keys

Reading guide

Q1 b · Q5 c · Q8 b · Q10 c. Sequence: ant works 1, grasshopper sings 2, autumn 3, warning 4, ignores 5, winter 6, knocks 7, leaves 8.

Assessment

Part A: lazy = not wanting to work · warn = tell someone something bad may happen · ignore = pay no attention · gather = collect · protect = keep safe · save = keep now, use later · autumn = season after summer · moral = the lesson · generous = happy to share · fable = short animal story with a lesson.

Part C: 1 b · 2 c · 3 b · 4 c · 5 b · 6 b · ★7 b · ★8 b (cicada).

Part D: worked · made · ignored · came · should have built/made · should have looked · should not have played · would work · ★used to play / would play · ★should not have been.

All pages accept small variations (contractions, either of two correct verbs) and are case-insensitive.
