Level 1 · The simple past

Notice it

The ant worked very hard. He looked for food.

The ant made a lovely house. "Save your food," said the ant.

Some past verbs add -ed. Some verbs change completely. You have to learn those.

Regular: work → worked  ·  play → played
Irregular: make → made  ·  say → said  ·  eat → ate
Negative: He did not (didn't) work. ← after did, the verb goes back to normal.

Level 2 · would and used to for repeated past actions

Notice it

Every day, the ant would work and look for bread or food.

Every time he got a piece of food, he would eat just a little.

The grasshopper would just sing and play music.

Here would does not mean the future. It means this happened again and again in the past.

would + base verb → a repeated past action: He would sing every day.
used to + base verb → a repeated past action or a past situation: He used to be lazy.
Careful: for something that happened one time, use the simple past: He knocked on the door. (NOT he would knock)

Level 3 · should have + past participle

Notice it

"You should have made a house," said the ant.

"You should have looked for food," replied the ant.

The ant is talking about the past. It is too late now. This is the grammar of regret and criticism — one of the most useful structures in English, and it is sitting right there in the last four lines of the fable.

should have + past participle → it was a good idea, but you did not do it.
should not have (shouldn't have) + past participle → you did it, but it was a bad idea.
In speaking it sounds like should've / shoulda. In writing, always: should have.
Compare: You should build a house. (now / future)  vs.  You should have built a house. (past — too late)
