Part 1 · Summer SUMMER

It was summer. The sun was hot.

The ant worked. He worked all day. He looked for food. He found food. He ate a little. He saved a lot.

The ant made a house. It was a good house.

The grasshopper did not work. He sang songs. He played music. He sat in the sun. He was happy.

1Once upon a time there was an ant and a grasshopper.

2The ant worked very hard. He worked and worked all day.

3The ant made a lovely house.

4Every day, the ant would work and look for bread or food.

5Every time he got a piece of food, he would eat just a little, then save the rest for winter.

6The grasshopper, on the other hand, was very lazy.

7The grasshopper liked to sing and relax every day. The grasshopper didn't work to save food.

8The grasshopper would just sing and play music. He would relax in the sun.

Long ago, in a field of tall grass, an ant and a grasshopper lived side by side.

All summer the ant labored. From sunrise until the light turned orange, she carried seeds and crumbs of bread — some of them twice her own size — down into the neat rooms of her house beneath the ground. Whenever she found food, she ate only a little and stored the rest away, because she knew what the cold months would ask of her.

The grasshopper, on the other hand, did nothing of the kind. He had a fiddle and a fine voice, and he used them. He sang to the heat and to the long afternoons. To anyone watching, he looked completely lazy.

Part 2 · Autumn AUTUMN

Then autumn came. The leaves fell. The days were short.

The ants gathered food. They worked hard.

The ant said, "Save your food! Winter is coming!"

The ant said, "Build a house!"

The grasshopper did not listen. He played music in the sun.

9Then autumn came. The leaves were falling.

10In autumn, the little ants were gathering food before there was no more. The ant worked very hard.

11"Save your food," warned the ant. But the grasshopper was lazy and didn't look for food.

12"Build a house. Winter is coming," said the ant. But again the grasshopper ignored the ant. He just relaxed and played music in the autumn sun.

Then autumn arrived. The leaves let go of the trees one by one, and the light came later each morning. Everywhere in the field, ants were gathering what was left before it disappeared.

"Save your food," the ant warned him. "Build a house. Winter is coming."

The grasshopper ignored her — twice. He stretched out in the last warm sun and went on playing, as if the season would hold still for him.

Then winter came. The days were cold. The sun was not enough.

The grasshopper was cold. He was hungry. He had no food. He had no house.

He went to the ant's house. He knocked on the door.

13Then the days grew cold. The grasshopper was unhappy and cold. The grasshopper needed a house to protect him from the winter.

14He knocked on the ant's door. "Please let me stay in your house for winter."

Then the days grew cold, and the sun was no longer enough. The grasshopper's music stopped. He had nothing to eat and nowhere to go, and nothing at all to protect him from the winter.

So he crossed the frozen field and knocked on the ant's door. "Please," he said, "let me stay in your house for the winter."

Part 4 · The Door THE ENDING

"Please, let me stay in your house," said the grasshopper.

"What did you do in the summer?" asked the ant.

"I played music. I relaxed in the sun," said the grasshopper.

"No. You can not come in. You should have built a house," said the ant.

"Please give me some food," said the grasshopper.

"No. You should have looked for food," said the ant.

The grasshopper walked away. He had no food and no house.

15"What were you doing all summer and autumn?" asked the ant.

16"Playing music and relaxing in the sun," said the grasshopper.

17"No, you can not come in. You should have made a house," said the ant.

18"Please give me some food at least," said the grasshopper.

19"No, you should have looked for food," replied the ant.

20The grasshopper left the ant's house. He had no food and no house for the winter.

"What were you doing all summer and autumn?" the ant asked.

"Playing music. Relaxing in the sun."

"Then no. You cannot come in. You should have built a house."

"Give me some food, at least."

"No. You should have looked for food."

The door closed. The grasshopper left with no food and no house for the winter.

The fable does not tell us what happened to him after that. It only tells us that he left.

In the French version by La Fontaine, the insect is a cicada, not a grasshopper — and the poem says plainly that the ant is not generous. That is the writer's opinion about the ant, not about the cicada.
