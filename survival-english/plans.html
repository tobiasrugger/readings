Getting Through the Day

Survival English · Unit 1 · Six lessons + assessment · Newcomer / low-beginner ELD · Mr. Toby

Unit overview

Everything a newcomer needs to survive the first three weeks of school in English.

Unit objective

Students can greet people, follow classroom directions, ask for what they need, describe where things are, tell time and read a schedule, and get help anywhere in the school.

Materials

  • Web unit: index.html — students sign in with name + school email, work saves automatically.
  • Printed worksheet: press Print in the unit toolbar. The level toggle controls what prints — set Easier English before printing the newcomer packet, Harder English before printing the low-beginner packet.
  • Wall posters: one per lesson, made by students during the We Do (butcher paper + markers).
  • Real classroom objects for Lessons 3 and 4: pencil, eraser, sharpener, dictionary, binder, water.
  • A blank school map for Lesson 6.

Assessment plan

  • Formative, daily: the app's Check my answers gives immediate right/wrong on every auto item. Dashboard shows per-item class percentages — reteach anything under 60%.
  • Formative, in the moment: the checkpoint box in each plan below. One quick oral or physical response, whole class.

  • Retake policy: the test locks after submit on that device. To reopen it for a student, have them clear the page data, or just reset the row in Supabase.

Day 1 · Hello — Greetings and Introductions

The first thing a newcomer must be able to do is say who they are without panicking.

I can greet people, introduce myself, and answer "How are you?"

Vocabulary (12)

hello / hi · good morning · good afternoon · goodbye / bye · See you later. · name · Nice to meet you. · How are you? · I'm fine, thank you. · I'm tired. · How do you spell it? · Excuse me.

Language you will use

  • Good morning. My name is Mr. Toby. What is your name?
  • Nice to meet you. How do you spell it?
  • Listen. Repeat. Say it to your partner.

Language students will use

My name is ______.
I am from ______.
Nice to meet you. / Nice to meet you too.
I'm fine, thank you. And you?

Do Now

Write your name on a folded card. Stand it on your desk. No English required yet.

I Do

Greet three students at the door, out loud, in front of everyone. Exaggerate. Then write the four-line exchange on the board and label the two speakers A and B. Model the BE table: I am / you are / he is. Point at yourself, at a student, at a third student.

We Do

Whole class chorally: "My name is ____." Then partner practice with the app dialogue, twice, switching roles. Then press Hide the second speaker and do it from memory. Two pairs perform for the class.

You Do

Practice items 1–8 in the app. Item 8 is the four-sentence self-introduction. Students who finish early do the Challenge box (informal version of the dialogue).

NEWCOMEREasier English on. Use the word bank and the frames. It is fine to copy the frame and change only the blank. Accept a single correct word for items 2–5.
LOW BEGINNERHarder English on. No bank. Item 8 must have four different sentences with correct BE forms and capital letters.
READY FOR MOREChallenge box: rewrite the dialogue as two students at lunch. Three lines must become informal.
CHECKPOINTWalk the room and ask five students, at random, "How are you?" Every student should answer without a pause. If more than two freeze, redo the choral drill tomorrow before the Do Now.

Homework

Introduce yourself to one adult at school who is not me. Write their name and their job.

Day 2 · Listen and Do — Following Teacher Directions

This is the lesson that makes every other lesson possible.

I can understand and follow classroom directions.

Vocabulary (13)

open / close · take out · put away · stand up / sit down · listen · repeat · circle · underline · raise your hand · line up · turn to page · be quiet · look at the board

Language you will use

  • Listen and do. Don't write. Just do it.
  • Please take out your binder. Turn to page twelve.
  • Watch me. Now you.

Language students will use

Please ______ your ______.
Don't ______.
Excuse me. I don't understand.

Do Now

Oral only: "Stand up. Sit down. Open your binder. Close your binder." Do it three times, faster each time. No paper.

I Do

Total Physical Response. Say the command, do the action, say it again. Then write it on the board and circle the verb. Show the contrast explicitly: You open your book crossed out, Open your book checked. Add please. Add Don't.

We Do

Simon Says with the thirteen commands. Students who sit out become the callers. Then the app dialogue with a partner, twice.

You Do

Practice items 1–8, including the listening item (headphones or whole-class playback). Item 8: write three real directions that start with a verb.

NEWCOMERGesture first, word second, writing last. Let them draw a tiny picture next to each vocabulary word. Accept one-word answers on the fill items.
LOW BEGINNERItem 5 must be rewritten as a full polite command. Add a negative command with Don't to item 8.
READY FOR MOREChallenge box: write five directions and run a partner through them without letting the partner see the paper.
CHECKPOINTGive five commands in a row with no gestures. Count how many students do all five. That number is your real comprehension data for the week.

Homework

Write four commands you heard today in any class. Start each one with a verb.

Day 3 · I Need Help — Expressions of Basic Need

A student who can ask for a pencil is no longer stuck.

I can ask for what I need and ask for help when I do not understand.

Vocabulary (12)

need · borrow · restroom / bathroom · pencil / pen / paper · eraser · pencil sharpener · dictionary · a drink of water · help · I don't understand. · Can you repeat, please? · I'm finished.

Language you will use

  • Please tell me if you need something. These are ways to say what you need.
  • What do you need? · Yes, you can. · No, you can't. Not now.
  • This student needs a pencil. The other students have a pencil.

Language students will use

Can I ______, please?
I need ______.
I don't understand. Can you repeat, please?

Do Now

Oral directions from Day 2, four commands, no gestures. Then: hold up a pencil and take it away from one student's desk.

I Do

Two-panel comic on the document camera. Panel one: a student with no pencil, everyone else has one. Write the teacher's bubble: What do you need? Students listen, repeat, copy. Panel two: I need a pencil. Can I borrow a pencil, please? Then Yes, you can. Repeat the whole cycle for paper, for help, for the restroom.

We Do

Partner reading of the app dialogue, twice, switching. Then in pairs, students make a dialogue poster for one of: pencil sharpener, dictionary, eraser, drink of water. Frontload the vocabulary. As many pairs as time allows perform. Posters go on the wall and stay up all year.

You Do

Practice items 1–8. Item 8 is a four-line dialogue with Excuse me and Thank you.

NEWCOMERLet them hold the real object while they say the sentence. Their poster can be mostly drawing with one written frame.
LOW BEGINNERPoster must have four lines and correct punctuation. Add the May I …? variant.
READY FOR MOREChallenge box: write a six-line dialogue where the answer is No with a polite reason, and the student asks for something else.
CHECKPOINTHold up four objects one at a time. Students call out the request sentence chorally. Then call on the three quietest students individually.

Homework

Ask one teacher for something today using a full sentence. Write what you said and what they answered.

Day 4 · What's in the Room — Objects and Places

Prepositions are invisible to newcomers until you make them physical.

I can name classroom objects and say where they are.

Vocabulary (13)

desk · chair · board · door / window · clock · backpack · notebook / binder · marker · trash can · shelf · in / on / under · next to / behind / in front of · between

Language you will use

  • Where is the marker? It's on the desk. Now it's under the desk.
  • There is one clock. There are thirty desks.
  • Point to it. Say it. Now write it.

Language students will use

There is a ______ ______ the ______.
There are ______ ______ in the classroom.
The ______ is next to the ______.

Do Now

Three requests from Day 3, spoken by three different students to the whole class.

I Do

Take one marker and move it: on the desk, under the desk, in the drawer, behind the computer, between two books. Say each sentence twice. Then build the there is / there are table on the board with real counts from the room. Teach a/an with the sound test: say a marker, an eraser and make them hear it.

We Do

Hide-and-say: one student hides an object while the class covers their eyes; the class asks Where is the ______? and the hider answers with a full preposition sentence. Then the app dialogue with a partner.

You Do

Practice items 1–9. Item 9 is four sentences about the real room using four different prepositions.

NEWCOMERGive them the preposition word bank as a strip on the desk. Let them point at the real object before each sentence.
LOW BEGINNERRequire two There is and two There are sentences and correct a/an throughout.
READY FOR MOREChallenge box: six sentences, six different prepositions, no repeats.
CHECKPOINTPut an object somewhere odd. Ask three students to describe where it is. Listen specifically for the preposition, not the noun.

Homework

Draw your room at home. Label six things. Write three sentences with prepositions.

Day 5 · What Time Is It — Time, Days, and Schedule

Half of newcomer tardies are a schedule-reading problem, not a behavior problem.

I can tell time, say the days, and talk about my class schedule.

Vocabulary (13)

o'clock · half past · quarter past / quarter to · minute / hour · early / late · period · schedule · lunch / break · days of the week · morning / afternoon / evening · A.M. / P.M.

Language you will use

  • What time is it? It's ______.
  • I have English at 9:15. I have lunch at 12:00.
  • At a time. On a day. In the morning.

Language students will use

It's ______ o'clock. / It's half past ______.
I have ______ at ______.
My ______ class is on ______.

Do Now

Four preposition sentences about the room, said out loud to a partner, no writing.

I Do

Use a real clock with movable hands, or draw one large. Do 1:00, 1:15, 1:30, 1:45 and say both forms of each. Then put the at / on / in chart on the board with three examples each and no explanation in English beyond the chart — let the pattern do the teaching.

We Do

Clock relay: call a time, partners race to draw it and say it. Then everyone pulls out their real schedule and reads one line of it out loud using the frame I have ______ at ______.

You Do

Practice items 1–9. Item 9 is four sentences about their own school day.

NEWCOMERDigital times only ("It's two thirty"). Skip quarter to. Give the days of the week as a printed strip.
LOW BEGINNERBoth forms required, including quarter to. All three prepositions used correctly.
READY FOR MOREChallenge box: write the full real schedule in complete sentences, using at, on, and in at least once each.
CHECKPOINTPoint at the classroom clock four times during the period and ask the class to answer chorally. The fourth time, call on individuals.

Homework

Copy your real schedule into your binder in full sentences. Bring it tomorrow.

Day 6 · Excuse Me — Getting Help at School

This one is a safety lesson as much as a language lesson.

I can find places in the school, ask for help, and say what is wrong.

Vocabulary (14)

main office · nurse · counselor · principal · security · cafeteria · library · gym · sick · hurt · lost · absent · late pass · emergency

Language you will use

  • If you are lost, sick, or scared, ask an adult. Start with Excuse me.
  • Where is the nurse's office? Where are the restrooms?
  • Who do you talk to if ______?

Language students will use

Excuse me. Where is the ______?
I don't feel well. Can I go to the ______?
I'm lost. How do I get to room ______?

Do Now

What time is it? Ask three students. Then: "What time is lunch? What time does school end?"

I Do

Blank school map on the document camera. Label each place as you say its name and what happens there. Build the question-word chart: where / what / when / who / why / how, one example each. Model Where is vs Where are with singular and plural places.

We Do

Walk the school if you can get the time — office, nurse, counselor, library, cafeteria. If you cannot, do the map version: students label a blank map in pairs, then quiz each other with Where is …? Then the app dialogue, twice, then with the second speaker hidden.

You Do

Practice items 1–9. Item 9 is a five-line role play about being lost.

NEWCOMERMap with pictures and words. Accept a two-line exchange for item 9 if it contains Excuse me and Thank you.
LOW BEGINNERFive full lines with correct question forms and at least one Where are.
READY FOR MOREChallenge box: eight-line role play solving three problems — late, lost, and unwell.
CHECKPOINTName a problem out loud ("Your head hurts." / "You need to change a class." / "You see a fight."). Students call out the right person. Do six.

Homework

Find the main office, the nurse, and your counselor's room. Write the room number of each.

Day 7 · Unit test

Thirty-two auto-scored points, two writing prompts, one class period.

Before the test

  • Set the level toggle with each student, not for the whole class. Easier English keeps the word banks; Harder English removes them and gives longer writing prompts.
  • Parts A–D are auto-scored the moment they press Submit. Part E lands in the dashboard for you.
  • The test locks on that device after submit, so students can't reopen and change answers.

What it covers

PartItemsWhat it measures
A — Vocabulary7Two matching sets, three multiple choice, two fill
B — Grammar14BE, imperatives, Can I, there is/are, a/an, prepositions, at/on/in, question forms
C — Conversation6Choosing the right thing to say in a real situation
D — Listening5Spoken directions, times, places, negatives
E — Writing2Self-introduction and a dialogue. Scored by you.

Writing rubric (4 points each, 8 total)

ScoreWhat it looks like
4Meets the full task. Complete sentences, correct BE / request forms, capital letters and end punctuation. Understandable on the first read.
3Meets the task. A few errors that do not block meaning.
2Partly meets the task, or relies entirely on copied frames. Meaning mostly clear.
1Fragments or single words. Some unit vocabulary present.
0Blank, or no English from this unit.

After the test

  • Open the dashboard, Unit test tab. Read Class by skill first. Any skill under 60% is a reteach, not a remediation.
  • Then read Item analysis. A single red item is usually a wording problem. Three red items in one skill is a teaching problem.
  • Export CSV for your gradebook. Writing responses are printed together at the bottom of the dashboard so you can score all of one prompt at once.
