2Why is it a poem?

Glaser never says "this is a poem." You know anyway. How?

If he had written prose
I never cheated again, Miss Lorenz, and I never stole another candy bar or money from the box in the top of my father's dresser, or from my mother's purse.
What he actually did
I never cheated again, Miss Lorenz. I never stole another candy bar or money from the box in the top of my father's dresser – or from my mother's purse.
Infer
Same words. What changes when you break them into lines?
Read both out loud. Where do you stop? What do you notice on the right that you skipped on the left?

3Turn the poem into a real letter

Glaser calls it a letter, but he never wrote it like one. Here is what a real letter has.

Date
March 14, 2026

Greeting
Dear Miss Lorenz,
Comma, not a colon. Then a new line.
Opening
I am writing because…
Say why you are writing, in the first sentence.
Body
On the day of the spelling test…
The story. What happened, in order.
Closing line
I want you to know that…
What it means to you now, years later.
Sign-off
Sincerely, / Yours truly, / With thanks,
Comma after it. Then your name below.

Now rewrite Glaser's poem as the letter he says it is. Same story, same feeling — letter format. Use full sentences and paragraphs.

4Now write your own

To a real person. The one you named in section 1, or anyone else.

Five things that make a thank you land

  1. Name the thing. Not "thanks for everything." Say "thanks for staying after school that Tuesday."
  2. Say what changed. "After that, I stopped being afraid to ask questions in class."
  3. Short is fine. Three true sentences beat ten polite ones.
  4. Late is fine. Glaser waited thirty years and it still worked.
  5. You do not have to send it. Researchers found that writing the letter made people happier even when they never mailed it. Sending it works better — but writing it is already most of the work.
Connect
Before you write: what is the one specific thing this person did? One moment, not a general habit.

5Backwards: turn an email into a poem

Glaser turned a thank-you into a poem. Now you do it. Here is how it works.

Watch it happen first

Ken sent his baseball coach this email. It is fine. It is also completely forgettable.

The email
Subject: thanks Hi Coach, I just wanted to say thanks for everything this season. I know I wasn't the best player on the team and I messed up a lot, especially in that game against Lincoln. But you kept putting me in anyway. I appreciate it. My mom says hi. See you next year I guess. Ken
The same email as a poem
I wasn't the best player. You knew it. Lincoln, fourth inning — I still see the ball going past my glove into the grass. You put me back in. That was all. You just put me back in.

What Ken did

  1. Cut the filler. "I just wanted to say," "I appreciate it," "my mom says hi" — all gone. None of it was the real thing.
  2. Kept the one concrete detail and made it bigger. "That game against Lincoln" became the ball, the glove, the grass. Poems want things you can see.
  3. Broke the line at the turn. "You put me back in" gets its own line and white space on both sides. That is the whole poem.
  4. Repeated the strongest line at the end. Saying it twice makes it mean more, not less.
  5. Changed nothing that was true. He added no new facts. He only chose which ones to keep.
Infer
Which single line of Ken's poem hits hardest? Why that one?

Now you try

Rosa wrote this email to a teacher. Cut it up and make it a poem.

Rosa's email
Subject: thank you Dear Ms. Nguyen, Hello, how are you. I am writing to thank you for helping me last year when I was new at this school and could not speak much English. You always talked slow for me and you never got angry when I did not understand. One time you stayed after school for one hour to help me with my essay. That was very kind. Anyway thank you very much for everything. Have a nice day. From, Rosa
Your job
Keep maybe a third of it. Find the one moment you can picture. Build the poem around that. Put the most important line alone, with space around it. End on the strongest word — not on "have a nice day."

Tap a piece to drop it into your poem. Skip the ones you do not want. You can type, delete, and rearrange freely.

Language
Give your poem a title. Not "Thank You."
Titles can be a place, a time, an object, or a line you cut out of the poem.
Infer
What did you cut, and why did it not belong?
Extension. Take the thank-you letter you wrote in section 4 and do the same thing to it. Cut two thirds. Keep the moment you can see. Then decide which version you will actually give the person — the letter or the poem. Either is a real answer.
