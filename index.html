1. Be impeccable with your word 💬

Impeccable means perfect, with no mistakes. Your words are powerful. Say what is true. Say what you mean. Don't gossip about other people. Don't say bad things about yourself, like "I am stupid."

Example: You make a promise, and you keep it.

2. Don't take anything personally 🛡️

Sometimes people are rude or unkind. Usually, this is about them: their bad day, their feelings, their problems. It is not about you. When you remember this, you don't feel hurt so much.

Example: A man yells at you on the bus. You think, "He is having a bad day. It is not about me."

3. Don't make assumptions ❓

An assumption is a guess that we think is true. We guess what other people think, and we are often wrong. Then we feel sad or angry for no reason. So don't guess. Ask questions. Say what you want, clearly.

Example: Your friend doesn't text back. Don't think, "She hates me." Ask, "Are you OK?"

4. Always do your best 💪

Try your best every day. Your best changes. When you are healthy and happy, your best is big. When you are sick or tired, your best is smaller, and that is OK. If you do your best, you don't need to feel bad about yourself later.

Example: You are tired, but you still try to finish your homework.

The Toltecs were people in ancient Mexico. They lived about 1,000 years ago. Their big city was Tula. Today, Tula is in the state of Hidalgo, north of Mexico City.

The Toltecs were builders, artists, and traders. In Tula there are four giant stone statues of warriors. Each statue is about 4.6 meters tall. You can still see them today.

Later, the Aztecs told many stories about the Toltecs. For the Aztecs, the Toltecs were very wise and very skillful. In the Aztec language, Nahuatl, the word toltecatl came to mean "artist" or "master builder."

In 1997, a Mexican writer, Don Miguel Ruiz, wrote a book called The Four Agreements. He says the ideas come from old Toltec teachings. His family taught him these teachings.

But there is a problem. Historians do not have old Toltec books with these four rules. So some people say the wisdom is ancient, and some people say it is new. What do you think? Maybe it does not matter. Maybe the question is: are the rules good?

Grammar: commands and how often

The four agreements are commands. A command tells someone what to do. It starts with a verb. There is no "you."

✅ Do it❌ Don't do it
Be honest.Don't gossip.
Ask questions.Don't make assumptions.
Always do your best.Don't take it personally.

Command: verb first. Negative command: Don't + verb. Never "Doesn't" and never "No" in a command.

C. How often?

Frequency words tell how often. They go before the verb: "I always do my best." But they go after am, is, are: "I am always honest."
