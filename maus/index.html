The question this unit asks

Whose stories get written down — and what happens to the ones that don’t?

Art Spiegelman’s father survived Auschwitz. That story only exists because a son spent years asking a difficult man questions. At the end of this unit you will do the same thing for one person in your own life, and we will publish it.

How the levels work

Every page has three levels at the top. They are not “easy, medium, hard” — they are how much scaffolding you want today. You can switch at any time and nobody can see which one you picked. Your level is saved per page, not per person, so you can be Level 1 for reading and Level 3 for the interview project.

Level 1

Pictures, yes/no, circle it, sentence frames. You can do every single activity in this unit at Level 1.

Level 2

Short answers with a sentence starter to get you going.

Level 3

Argument, evidence, counter-argument. No frames.

Every reading guide has a “four heads, one answer” task. Each person at the table gets a different piece of the puzzle, and one of the four pieces needs no English reading at all. Nobody can finish it alone. That is on purpose.

If this gets heavy

This unit is about a real genocide, and some of you have lived through war or left a country in a hurry. There is a “I need a minute” button in the corner of every page. Press it and it tells you your options. Nothing is recorded. You never have to explain.

You can also tell me on any exit ticket by choosing “This was heavy for me today.” I read those first.
