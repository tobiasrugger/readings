Lesson 1 — Do I Even Have Anything to Put on a Résumé?

Part A. The big question

Most students say: "I have never had a job. I have nothing to write." That is almost never true.

A résumé is not only paid work. It is a list of things you have done responsibly for other people. Read the list below. Tap every one you have done.

Why this matters

Work is work. If you did it, you can write it.

Why this matters

Employers are looking for evidence that you show up, follow directions, and take care of things. Unpaid work is evidence. Work in another country is evidence. Work for your own family is evidence.

Why this matters

A résumé is an argument, not a list. Every line is evidence for one claim: "You should hire me." Unpaid and family work is legitimate evidence — the job of the résumé writer is to describe it in the language employers already recognize. That is what the rest of this unit teaches you to do.

Part B. Action verbs

A résumé never says "I helped." It says "Assisted." Strong past-tense verbs do the work. Match each strong verb to the simple word that means the same thing.
