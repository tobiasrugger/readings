Lesson 2 — Sections & One-Liners

Part A. Which section?

Every fact on a résumé lives in exactly one section. Put each item where it belongs.

Part B. What order?

Reverse chronological means newest first, oldest last. Number these jobs 1 (top of the résumé) to 5 (bottom).

Remember

New at the top. Old at the bottom.

Why?

Employers read the top of the page first and often stop there. Your newest work is usually your strongest, so it goes where their eyes land.

Why?

Recruiters spend roughly six to eight seconds on a first pass. Reverse chronological order is a convention that puts your most current evidence in that window. There are other formats — functional and hybrid résumés group by skill instead of date — but they are used to hide gaps, and for a high school student they usually raise more questions than they answer. Stick with reverse chronological.

Part C. One-liner surgery

Each line below is broken. Rewrite it. The checker will tell you what is still wrong.

3 rules:
1. Start with a past-tense verb.
2. No "I".
3. No period at the end.
The formula: Past-tense verb + what + how much / how many
"Served customers" → "Served 60+ customers per shift". Numbers make it real.
The formula: Past-tense verb + object + scale + result
Weakest: "Helped customers." Better: "Served 60+ customers per shift." Strongest: "Served 60+ customers per shift while training two new hires." The third version does two jobs in one line — it shows the task and the trust placed in you.

Part D. Now write your own

Think about one real thing you have done — a job, a chore, a team, translating for your family. Write four one-liners about it. The checker runs as you type.

When all four are green, copy them into your résumé page.
