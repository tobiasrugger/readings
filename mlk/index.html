PARAGRAPH 1

Reverend Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. was born in Georgia in 1929. He was a good student. After high school, Martin Luther King, Jr. went to college to become a preacher. He became a great speaker and a famous American leader. He was very good at making speeches and speaking in front of other people. When he spoke, he inspired the audience. He spoke loudly and clearly and often used metaphors and similes. People liked listening to him.

PARAGRAPH 2

Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. was one of the important leaders of the civil rights movement. “Civil rights” means rights all people (citizens) of a country should have. In the 1960s, some of the laws in the United States were racist. African-American (Black) people did not have the same rights as white people. For example, African-American people could not drink the same water as white people. African-American people had to drink from a different water fountain than white people. Also, African-American people had to sit in the back of a bus. It was illegal for an African-American person to sit in the front of a bus. The police could arrest an African-American person who wanted to sit in the front of a bus.

PARAGRAPH 3

Dr. King wanted to make things equal for people regardless of their race, or color of their skin. Dr. King worked hard to fight for rights for nonwhite people. Dr. King was a minister in a church. He made many speeches and he gave sermons in his church about what was and was not fair. When people listened to Dr. King’s speeches, they learned about and understood the racism and the problems with civil rights in the United States. When people listened to Dr. King talk, they often decided to protest against the racism in the United States.

Question 5 Check for understanding

PARAGRAPH 4

Dr. King believed that it was important to fight for what was right without using violence. When someone fights or protests for rights without violence, this is called nonviolent protest. Dr. King organized and participated in many nonviolent protests. In some protests, people marched in the street and sang songs or demanded more rights. In other protests, called sit-ins, people would sit in a place where white people or the police did not want them to be. They wouldn’t leave for hours or days. They would not do anything violent, they would only sit there.

PARAGRAPH 5

These protests persuaded many Americans that the civil rights movement was right. Many people saw how Black Americans were treated badly in other parts of the country and felt shocked. Dr. King was a powerful leader and many people liked him. He had many followers. However, not everybody liked him. Some people even hated him. Some people thought Dr. King was dangerous. One day, in 1968, he was assassinated, or murdered, in Tennessee. Millions of people in the United States were sad to lose Dr. King, a brilliant, nonviolent leader. If Dr. King had not been assassinated, the United States might be an even better country today.

PARAGRAPH 6

Dr. King’s grave is at the King Center in the city of Atlanta, Georgia. People in the United States remember Martin Luther King for his famous speeches, for his strong principles, and for his hard, tireless work to create more equality and peace in the United States. The people of the United States honor Martin Luther King, Jr. every year in January. We have a national holiday, called Martin Luther King, Jr. Day. The holiday is celebrated on the third Monday in January.
