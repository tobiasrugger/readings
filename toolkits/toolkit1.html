TOOLKIT #1

Pronouns

A pronoun is a short word that takes the place of a name. Instead of saying Maria, Maria, Maria, you say she. Keep this open in science, history, math, art, music, or PE.

1The seven people

These seven pronouns do the action. They come before the verb.

WhoPronounIt meansExampleMy language (ML)
I the person speaking — youI am a student.
you the person you are talking toYou are my partner.
he one boy or manHe is my brother.
she one girl or womanShe is my teacher.
it one thing, or an animalIt is a pencil.
we me + other peopleWe are in period 3.
they two or more people or thingsThey are my friends.

In English there is only one word for you. It is the same for one friend and for the whole class. Many languages have two or three. Write yours in the box.

2The same person, four jobs

The word changes depending on where it sits in the sentence. Read across each row — it is all the same person.

Does the action → goes before the verb. She measured the beaker.
Receives the action → goes after the verb or after to, for, with. The teacher helped her. Give the beaker to her.
Owns a thing → a noun comes right after it. That is her beaker.
Owns it alone → no noun after it. That beaker is hers.
Two words people mix up.
its = it owns something. The plant lost its leaves.
it's = it is. It's a warm day.
If you can say it is, use the apostrophe. If not, don't.

3he, she, it, or they?

If you are talking about…UseExample
one boy or manhe / him / hisMr. Toby lost his keys.
one girl or womanshe / her / hersMs. Lee raised her hand.
one thing, place, or animalit / itsThe cell divided. It grew.
two or more of anythingthey / them / theirThe students packed their bags.
one person, and you do not know if they are a boy or a girlthey / them / theirSomeone left their jacket.

The last row is normal, correct English. Use they for one person when you do not know, or when the person asks you to.

5Before or after the verb?

Choose the pronoun that fits.

1. The coach chose ___ for the team. (me / I)
2. ___ finished the lab report. (Me / I)
3. Please give the ruler to ___. (her / she)
4. ___ sing in the choir together. (Us / We)
5. The teacher helped ___ with the equation. (them / they)
6. ___ is my best friend. (Him / He)

Trick that always works: take the other person out of the sentence. The coach chose Ana and me → The coach chose me. Sounds right, so me is right.
