# The Odyssey — ELD Unit Plan

**Mr. Toby · Galileo Academy of Science and Technology**
Seven weeks · newcomers through low-intermediate · three reading levels in one app

---

## The essential question

**Was Odysseus a good person?**

Everything in the unit feeds that question. Each chapter carries one ethical problem, students argue it in discussion, the discussions become a debate, and the debate becomes the essay. Nobody writes the essay cold.

| Chapter | Ethical question | Greek idea |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Trojan War | When is it right to go to war? | Cost and gain |
| 2. The Lotus Eaters | Happiness today, or for a whole life? | *eudaimonia* vs. *hēdonē* |
| 3. Scylla, Charybdis, Helios | Is it ever right to lie to protect people? | The moral dilemma |
| 4. Kalypso | Can a person learn to love? | *eros, philos, agape, storge* |
| 5. Odysseus's Return | Is revenge ever right? | Moral relativism |

---

## The three levels

Students choose their level in the green bar and can change it any time. The app remembers the choice and reports it to the dashboard.

**Easier** — rewritten text in short sentences with high-frequency vocabulary. Every question has sentence frames. Recall and comprehension questions appear that the other levels don't get.

**Standard** — the full adapted text. Frames available but not pushed.

**Challenge** — the full text plus a *language close-up* box on one sentence per chapter, questions that ask students to evaluate the writer's choices rather than the character's, and sentence frames hidden behind a toggle instead of offered by default.

The text differs at Easier; the *task* differs at all three. That's deliberate — a newcomer should still be arguing about whether revenge is right.

## Weekly sequence

### Week 1 — Context and the Trojan War

**Day 1** · LO: I can take notes from text and images. CO: I can learn about the context of *The Odyssey*.
Do Now: What did you read this weekend? For how long?
Gallery walk of ancient Greece. Jeopardy. Open the app together and set up logins.
Reflection: Write two things you learned about ancient Greece.

**Day 2** · LO: I can preview the characters and places in *The Odyssey*.
Do Now: Where is *The Odyssey* from? How old is it?
Opening stanza sort. Flocabulary rap — listen, read, code for names and places, build a class Characters and Settings chart.
Reflection: How do you feel about starting this book?

**Day 3** · Movie Part 1 (0:00–27:00) with the movie guide in the app.
Do Now: What is *The Odyssey* about?

**Day 4** · LO: I can read the first chapter. CO: I can use reading strategies.
Do Now: What are some reasons why people start wars? At least two.
Read Chapter 1 in the app. Before-reading box, then the text, then the reading guide.
Reflection: on the reading protocol.

**Day 5** · LO: I can use discussion language to say my idea and add to others' ideas.
Do Now: I would go to war if ______.
Discussion: Why did the Greeks go to war with Troy? When is it right to go to war?
Reflection: Write down a good idea a classmate had.

---

### Week 2 — Happiness and the Lotus Eaters

**Day 6** · Grammar gym workout 1 (past simple) — this is the narrative tense they need for every chapter. Ten minutes of the gym, then finish Chapter 1 questions.

**Day 7** · LO: I can write my own definition of happiness.
Do Now: What is happiness?
Open **What is happiness?** in the app. Students write their own experience first, then read Aristotle and Epicurus, then sort their own examples. Posters on the wall.

**Day 8** · LO: I can read the next chapter. CO: I can connect the Lotus Eaters to Greek ideas about happiness.
Do Now: Look at the happiness posters. Which idea do you like more? Why?
Read Chapter 2. **Stop at the split debate** — the app pauses the text and asks whether Odysseus should drink. List reasons for and against as a class, students pick a side and physically move, then read the ending.

**Day 9** · Do Now: What makes you happy?
Discussion: Is it more important to be happy for today or happy for life? What does Odysseus think? Do you agree?
Start the happiness paragraph.

**Day 10** · Do Now: Look at the posters. Write one idea you agree with.
Paragraph writing. Grammar gym workout 2 (because / so) as a warm-up — they need it for the paragraph.

---

### Week 3 — The Cyclops, Circe, and the dilemma

**Day 11** · Movie Part 2 (30:00–1:07:00) — Cyclops and Circe. These episodes are movie-only, so the guide carries more weight here.

**Day 12** · Do Now: If something bad is going to happen, do you want to know in advance or be surprised?
Read Chapter 3 to the **stop point**. Students take a side on whether Odysseus should tell his men, then read the rest.

**Day 13** · Grammar gym workouts 3 and 4 (if + will; should / should have). These two carry the ethics vocabulary — *should have told* is the sentence they will need all unit.

**Day 14** · LO: I can use the language of discussion. CO: I can discuss Odysseus's dilemma.
Do Now: What was Odysseus's dilemma?
Discussion: Why didn't he tell his men? Should he have? Follow-ups: What would have happened if he told them? Is he being honest? Is honesty important here? Does he trust his men? Is trust important?
Cross the Line: *If you were going to die tomorrow, would you want to know today?*

**Day 15** · Movie Part 3 (1:07:00–1:40:00). Compare the film's Scylla with the chapter — good evidence for the essay.

---

### Week 4 — Love and Kalypso

**Day 16** · Do Now: What is love?
Open **What is love?** — students write first, then learn *eros, philos, agape, storge*, then sort their own examples into the four.

**Day 17** · Do Now: What did you read this weekend?
Read Chapter 4. The four-loves sort is inside the reading guide, so it recycles yesterday's vocabulary immediately.

**Day 18** · Grammar gym workout 5 (reported speech). Chapter 4 is almost all dialogue, so the examples come straight from what they just read.

**Day 19** · Do Now: What did Kalypso want? What did Odysseus want?
Discussion: Should Odysseus learn to love Kalypso? Is it possible to learn to love someone you don't love at first? Follow-ups: Can you control who you love? How would Kalypso know if he really loved her? How do you know if someone loves you?

**Day 20** · Do Now: Is it possible to learn to love?
Paragraph about love.

---

### Week 5 — Revenge and the homecoming

**Day 21** · Do Now: What will Odysseus do when he gets home and finds the suitors?
Movie Part 4 — the homecoming.

**Day 22** · Do Now: Explain what revenge is.
Read Chapter 5. Longest chapter of the unit — give it the whole period.

**Day 23** · Grammar gym workout 6 (past perfect). Chapter 5 is built on backstory, so this is the chapter where *had* + past participle finally makes sense.

**Day 24** · Do Now: Who did Odysseus take revenge on?
Discussion: Why and how did he take revenge? Was he right? If not, what should he have done? Follow-ups: When is revenge the right thing? Can revenge make things better? What would happen to Odysseus in the USA today? Can an action be right in one society and wrong in another?

**Day 25** · Mind maps. Gallery walk. This is the harvest — every discussion from five weeks goes onto the wall as evidence.

---

### Week 6 — The debate

**Day 26** · LO: I can write a speech for our debate.
Do Now: What did you read this weekend?
Assign teams and the eight roles. Gallery walk of mind maps. Teams agree on their main points and build a reference poster. Open **The debate** in the app — the evidence bank shows both sides, so nobody is arguing from nothing.

**Day 27** · LO: I can make an argument and support it with evidence.
Do Now: What is a good person?
Write speeches in the app. Make notecards. Practice. Grammar gym workout 7 (opinion and evidence language) as the warm-up.

**Day 28** · Practice round. Team leaders check that no two speeches repeat each other.

**Day 29** · **Debate.** Two minutes per speaker — the app has a timer. Non-debating students take notes on the listening sheet.

**Day 30** · Debrief. What was the strongest argument you heard, and why?

---

### Week 7 — The essay

**Day 31** · Essay idea organization. Open the **Essay planner**. Students fill H, O, T only.

**Day 32** · Body paragraph 1: P, E, A, E, A. The word counter tells them when a box is too thin.

**Day 33** · Body paragraph 2. Then R and C.

**Day 34** · Press **Put it together**, copy the four paragraphs into a document, type and revise.

**Day 35** · Lab. Revision against the rubric. Conferences.

**Unit test** whenever it fits — 20 auto-scored questions and three written responses. Students may use their reading guides.

---

## Assessment

**Formative, continuous.** Every box in the app saves to Supabase every 2.5 seconds. The dashboard shows completion percentage, auto-scored accuracy, and the full text of every written answer, per student, sortable by period.

**Auto-scored** — 30 multiple-choice items across the chapters, movie guides, and unit test, plus 39 grammar gym items. Accepts multiple correct forms (contractions, alternate word order) and ignores punctuation and case.

**Teacher-scored** — around 100 written responses, grouped in the dashboard by chapter, movie, ethics, grammar production, debate, essay, and test.

**Summative** — the debate (speech and questions) and the essay, on the HOTPEARC rubric built into the app: argument with evidence, organization, grammar and spelling, revision.

The unit test is a check, not the grade. The essay is the grade.

## Printing

The Print button opens a menu:

- This page
- All five reading guides — text, word lists, and questions at the current level, with writing space
- Grammar gym
- Essay planner and rubric
- Debate task cards
- Unit test
- The whole packet

Print at the **Easier** level for a newcomer packet and at **Standard** for everyone else. The word list prints as a table under each chapter automatically.
