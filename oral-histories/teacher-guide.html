How do we connect with our family?

Unit overview

Length: 15 instructional days (~50 min), flexible to 12 or 18.
Level: Newcomers and low-beginners, with an Easier/Harder toggle built into every task.
Culminating product: each student interviews a family member or elder, then writes a 350–600 word oral history. All pieces become one class book.

ListeningSpeakingReadingWritingGrammar: past tenseQuestion formation

Why this unit

Newcomers arrive with a family history nobody at school has ever asked about. This unit makes that history the curriculum. The interviewing skills — open questions, follow-ups, listening through silence — transfer to every class, every job interview, and every conversation they will have in English.

Materials

  • Teacher dashboard: dashboard.html — live progress, auto-scores, full answers, CSV.
  • StoryCorps recordings + transcripts — you publish these from inside the app (Section 4 → "Teacher: set up stories").

Suggested StoryCorps pieces (all short, all animated versions exist): Germans in the Woods, A Mother's Tough Standards, Facundo the Great, Miss Devine, Q&A, Eyes on the Stars. Choose two or three. At least one should center an immigrant family. Paste each transcript into the app so students read while they listen.

Day by day

Day 1 — What is an oral history?

App Section 1 · Launch
Objective: I can explain what an oral history is and name one object that connects me to my family.
  • Hook (10 min): Bring in three objects of your own — one with a story, two without. Hold each up. "What do you see?" Then tell the story behind the one that has one. Students feel the difference between an object and a story.
  • Mini-lesson (8 min): Oral history = a true story told out loud and recorded. Nobody writes it first. Introduce the unit vocabulary in the app.
  • Work (20 min): Section 1 — sketch your object, write about it, agree/disagree statements, quickwrite.
  • Share (10 min): Three volunteers describe their object. Announce the project: you will interview someone in your family.
Send home tonight: "Think about who you might interview." No writing. Just the question.

Day 2 — Question words

App Section 2 · Grammar + oral practice
Objective: I can choose the right question word and put a past-tense question in the correct order.
  • Do Now (5): Post the question-word chart. Choral read.
  • Mini-lesson (10): Question word + did + person + base verb. Break the did you went error explicitly — it will show up all unit.
  • Work (25): Section 2 A and B (auto-scored). Circulate; the dashboard shows you who is stuck in real time.
  • Speak (10): Stand up, find a partner, ask three questions using three different question words. Switch partners twice.
Extension: Make the question-word chart a poster for content-area classrooms. Other teachers will use it.

Day 3 — Open, closed, follow-up

App Section 3 · The core skill of the unit
Objective: I can tell an open question from a closed one and write a follow-up question.
  • Demonstration (12): Ask a willing student only closed questions for two minutes. Let it die. Then re-interview the same student with open questions and follow-ups. Ask the class what changed.
  • Mini-lesson (8): Closed gets facts. Open gets stories. Follow-up gets the truth.
  • Work (25): Section 3 A–D. Use "Show model answers" only after they have tried.
  • Exit (5): Every student writes one open question about the person next to them and asks it.

Days 4–6 — Listening to real oral histories

App Section 4 · One story per day · Predict → Listen → Respond
Objective: I can listen to an oral history, answer the 5 Ws, and summarize it for a partner.
  • Before (10): Vocabulary hunt — four words students find in the transcript and guess. Then predict from the title and first two lines only.
  • Listen (10): Play it once with no reading. Play it again while they read the transcript. Play it a third time if the room wants it — they usually do.
  • Respond (20): Listening guide, then the "go deeper" questions. The feeling question is the one that matters.
  • Write—Pair—Share (10): Write your summary, read it to a partner, then write what they said. The app has a box for this.
Station option: if you would rather compress this, set up three laptops as stations and let groups rotate through three stories in two days. Each station has its own slot in the app (Story 1–4), so all four can be live at once.
Handle with care: some of these stories involve war, death, or separation. Say so before you press play, and say that anyone may step out. Several of your students have their own version of these stories.

Days 5–7 — Past tense toolkit

App Section 5 · Runs alongside the listening days
Objective: I can use the simple past — regular, irregular, and was/were — to tell about the past.
  • 15 minutes a day, not a whole period. Day 5: was/were + regular -ed rules. Day 6: the irregular list, Practice A and C. Day 7: past-tense questions and reported speech.
  • Practice A–D are auto-scored; the dashboard breaks the score out by set so you can see exactly which pattern is failing.
  • Section F is the transfer task: five sentences about their own memory, all in the past.
Reported speech is the piece that makes the final writing possible. If they cannot turn "I am the oldest of seven" into she said she was the oldest of seven, their oral history will be all quotation marks.

Days 8–9 — Planning the interview

App Section 6
Objective: I can choose a narrator, select ten good questions, write three of my own, and plan my follow-ups.
  • Day 8: Choose the person. Choose ten questions from the Great Questions bank (it filters by relationship). Write three original open questions — the app checks whether they are actually open.
  • Day 9: Follow-up planning table. Logistics checklist. H.A.V.E. Then print the interview sheet — there is a button that prints only that page.
Say this out loud: interview in whatever language you and your narrator share. Record in that language. Translate only the parts you write down. The story matters more than the English, and the writing will be better for it.
If a student has no family here — and some will not — offer alternates without making it a conversation in front of the class: an older neighbor, a staff member, a coach, a person from their community center, or a video call with someone overseas. The "For a neighbor" question set exists for exactly this.

Day 10 — Mock interview

App Section 7
Objective: I can conduct a five-minute interview and take feedback on it.
  • Three rounds of five minutes: interview, switch, switch back and fix one thing.
  • Partner scores the four-row rubric on the interviewer's device. This is the rehearsal that prevents a wasted real interview.
  • Close by having two pairs perform 90 seconds in front of the room.
The interview happens over the weekend or across the next few nights. Give a hard date. Remind them to ask permission before recording.

Days 11–14 — Writing the oral history

App Section 8
Objective: I can write an oral history with a title, an introduction, one story told in detail, two direct quotes, and a reflection.
  • Day 11: Quote punctuation mini-lesson (Section 8A), then title and introduction. Model your own on the board first.
  • Day 12: Background and the story. Push the whole class toward one story told slowly rather than a whole life told fast.
  • Day 13: Reflection, then the revision checklist. Peer read: partners find the two quotes and check the punctuation.
  • Day 14: Final edit, read-aloud pass, optional "in their voice" first-person rewrite. Word count target 350–600.
Differentiation is already in the file: Easier shows fill-in frames for every paragraph; Harder replaces them with craft prompts. Toggle is in the green bar and it saves per student.

Day 15 — Assessment and publication

App Section 9
Objective: I can show what I learned about questioning, past tense, and oral history.
  • Part A: 12-point auto-scored quiz.
  • Part B: 16-point rubric, self-scored; you score the same rubric in the dashboard.
  • Part C: reflection, including a return to the Day 1 agree/disagree statements — did anything change?
  • Publish: print all pieces, bind the class book, and read three aloud. If students want to illustrate their piece as a comic, let them — it pairs naturally with a Maus unit.

Scaffolds built in

  • Easier / Harder toggle on every section — sentence frames vs. craft prompts, four items vs. six.
  • Tap-to-translate on all unit vocabulary, in Spanish, Chinese, Arabic, Vietnamese, Thai, and Nepali.
  • Sentence frames for every extended response.
  • Model answers revealed on demand, after attempting.
  • Immediate feedback on every auto-scored item.
  • Printable blank packet for students without a device, or for the day the wifi dies.
  • Interview in any language — the product is the story, not the English of the interview.

Tech setup (once)

Run this in the Supabase SQL editor before the first day. The anon key cannot create tables, so this has to be done in the dashboard by hand.

create table if not exists oralhistory_work (
  id            bigserial primary key,
  email         text not null,
  name          text,
  period        text,
  home_language text,
  data          jsonb not null default '{}'::jsonb,
  score         int,
  max_score     int,
  turned_in     boolean default false,
  updated_at    timestamptz default now()
);
-- plain-column unique index; on_conflict=email depends on this exactly
create unique index if not exists oralhistory_work_email_key
  on oralhistory_work (email);

create table if not exists oral_history_texts (
  slug       text primary key,
  title      text,
  media_url  text,
  raw        text,
  updated_at timestamptz default now()
);

alter table oralhistory_work   enable row level security;
alter table oral_history_texts enable row level security;
create policy oh_work_anon on oralhistory_work
  for all to anon using (true) with check (true);
create policy oh_text_anon on oral_history_texts
  for all to anon using (true) with check (true);
The email column must be a plain column in the unique index — not lower(email). The app already lowercases the address before it sends, so a plain index is correct and the upsert will actually fire. An expression index here fails silently.
