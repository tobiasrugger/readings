1What is an oral history?

Day 1 · Launch · 50 minutes

An oral history is a true story that one person tells out loud to another person, who listens and records it. Nobody writes it first. It is a memory, spoken.

Every family carries stories that were never written down. When nobody asks, the stories disappear. In this unit you will learn how to ask — and then you will interview someone in your own family.

Our big project: You will interview a family member, record their story, and write it as a real oral history. All of our oral histories will become one class book.

3Open, closed, and follow-up questions

Day 3 · The single most important interviewing skill.

Closed question

A question with a short answer — usually yes, no, or one word.

Did you like school? → “Yes.”
What is your favorite color? → “Blue.”
How old were you? → “Ten.”

Open question

A question with a long answer. You cannot answer it in one word.

What do you remember about school?
What was the hardest part of leaving?
Tell me about the day you arrived.

Follow-up question

A question that builds on the answer you just heard. This is how a short answer becomes a story.

You: Did you like school?  —  Them: “Yes.”
You: What did you like about it?  —  Them: “My teacher, Mrs. Diaz. She let me stay inside at lunch and read.”
You: Why did you want to stay inside?  —  …and now you have a real story.

The rule of the unit: Closed questions get facts. Open questions get stories. Follow-up questions get the truth.
A closed question has a ______ answer. An open question has a ______ answer.

E. Reporting what someone said

When you write an oral history, you will report what the person told you.
They said: “I am the oldest of seven.” → You write: She said that she was the oldest of seven.
They said: “We have nothing.” → You write: He said that they had nothing.
They said: “I can still hear it.” → You write: She said that she could still hear it.

H.A.V.E. — how you show up

Handshake or greeting — start warmly.

Attitude — smile, be open, be curious.

Voice — speak slowly and clearly.

Eye contact — look at them, not at your paper.

Interview manners: Start by saying the date, your name, their name, and how you are related — that is how every StoryCorps interview begins. Do not interrupt. Let silence happen — the best answers come after a pause. Ask them to spell names and places. Say thank you at the end.
Opening script: “Today is ______. My name is ______. I am here with my ______, ______. Thank you for talking with me.”

A. Punctuating a quote (you will need this)

“I was the only one who finished school,” she said.
She said, “I was the only one who finished school.”

Three rules: capital letter to start the quote · comma before or inside the closing quotation mark · the period goes inside the quotation marks.
Directions: Punctuate these correctly.
