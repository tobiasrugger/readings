Writing Workshop · The PEA Paragraph

I can write a paragraph with a clear point, connected evidence, and real analysis.

The Three Parts

PPoint — the first sentence. It tells the main idea of the whole paragraph. Also called the topic sentence.
EEvidence — examples that show the main idea. From a class text, from your life, or from the world.
AAnalysis — the sentence that explains why the evidence matters.

PEA = 1 evidence.   PEEA = 2 evidence.   PEEEA = 3 evidence. Your level tells you which one to write.

LevelWriteEvidence typeTarget length
EasierPEAYour own life, or a retold moment from the story4–5 sentences

Model Paragraph

Mr. Toby’s paragraph about his three selves. Find the P, the E’s, and the A.

After learning about my three selves in English class, I understand more about who I am. The important information about my actual self is that I am calm but sometimes I get a little cranky. An example from my life is that today I felt annoyed by students who had a lot of energy and were not focused on school. In comparison, my ideal self is always calm and patient. An example is that I want to use a patient tone with students rather than a sharp one. Finally, my ought self is to be a teacher who is so respected that students are always 100% focused. For example, every student should be in his or her chair when the bell rings. It’s important to understand my different selves because now I can see what I want to be and try to be closer to it every day.

Step 2 · Build Your Point

Choose one and copy it. Change the words in brackets.

• After learning about [topic] in English class, I understand more about [idea].

• My actual, ideal, and ought selves show several interesting sides of me.

• In “[story],” the [character]’s actual self is very different from his ideal self and his ought self.

• The different influences on my life help make me the person I am today.

Write your own. A strong point makes a claim someone could disagree with. “The boy has three selves” is a fact. “The boy’s ought self causes him more pain than his ideal self” is a point.

Evidence sentence starters: An example from my life is ______ . · An example from the text is ______ . · In comparison, ______ . · For example, ______ . · Finally, ______ .

Quoting. Use quotation marks and keep it short. The boy calls the jacket his “green ugly brother,” which shows he cannot separate it from himself. Never drop a quote alone as its own sentence.

Step 4 · Write Your Analysis

Analysis is not a summary. It answers: so what? Why does this evidence matter?

• These three selves show that ______ .
• It is important to understand my different selves because ______ .
• In the future, I can use this information to ______ .
• These influences are important in my life because ______ .

At Challenge level your analysis must use a unit concept by name: gap, self-esteem, magnitude, peer pressure, collaborative culture, ought self.
