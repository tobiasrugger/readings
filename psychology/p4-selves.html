Easier

Every person has three selves.

1. The actual self. This is who you really are today. Your real characteristics, right now.

2. The ideal self. This is who you want to be. The person you wish to become.

3. The ought self. This is who you think you should be. It is what other people expect from you: your family, your teachers, your friends.

People compare their three selves. The comparison changes how they feel.

Actual vs. Ideal. A big gap → sad, disappointed, frustrated. A small gap → happy, good self-esteem.

Actual vs. Ought. A big gap → worried, guilty, anxious. A small gap → calm and relaxed.

Standard & Challenge

Imagine you are talking to a friend about your life. You probably use the words “I,” “me,” and “myself” a lot. But what do you mean when you use those words? Who are you?

One important psychological theory says that every person has three selves.

The actual self is the person you really are. It is all your personality factors and characteristics that you really have, right now, today.

The ideal self is the person you want to be. It is all the characteristics you wish to have, but do not really have right now.

The ought self is the person you think you should be. It is all the characteristics that you think other people want you to have.

Psychologists say that people often compare their actual self to their ideal self and their ought self. When people make these comparisons, it changes how they feel.

Actual vs. Ideal Self. If a person’s actual self is very different from their ideal self, that person may feel disappointed, sad, frustrated, or depressed, and may have low self-esteem. If the two are very similar, the person may feel happy and have high self-esteem.

Actual vs. Ought Self. If a person’s actual self is very different from their ought self, that person may feel worried, guilty, anxious, or afraid, and may have low self-esteem. If the two are similar, the person may feel calm and relaxed.

Notice the difference between the two feelings. The ideal gap makes you feel sad — something is missing. The ought gap makes you feel afraid — something is wrong and someone will notice. That is why a student can be disappointed about a grade and, separately, terrified about telling a parent. Two gaps, two feelings, one report card.
