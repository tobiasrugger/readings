Farewell to Manzanar · Unit Plan

Course: ELD · newcomers and low-beginners  ·  Length: 20 instructional days  ·  Mr. Toby · Galileo Academy of Science and Technology

Texts: Farewell to Manzanar by Jeanne Wakatsuki Houston and James D. Houston (adapted chapters 1 and 2, two reading levels) · Farewell to Manzanar (1976 film, dir. John Korty) · Executive Order 9066 (excerpt).

Essential question: What does a person lose when a country decides they do not belong — and what can they keep?

What students will be able to do

StrandBy the end of the unit
ReadingRead an adapted memoir chapter and answer literal and inferential questions with text evidence.
Listening / viewingWatch a feature film in segments and take structured notes on specific scenes.
SpeakingState and defend a position in a dyad and in a whole-class line-up.
WritingWrite a multi-paragraph response with a claim, evidence from two media, and explanation.
LanguageUse the simple past (regular and irregular), negative past, subject and object pronouns, there was / there were, and because / so accurately in writing about a past narrative.
ELD standardsPI.A.1, PI.A.3, PI.B.6, PI.B.7, PI.C.10, PI.C.11 · PII.A.1, PII.B.3, PII.C.6

Day by day

Day 1 — Enter the question

Anticipation guide (hub) · Manzanar photo study · map of the ten camps

Students open the unit hub and complete the Before column of the anticipation guide, then write on the one statement they feel most strongly about. Whole-class stand-up line: agree side of the room / disagree side. No reading yet — the point is to get a position on record so they can be surprised later.

MaterialsHub · anticipation guide
Student productWritten stance on one statement

Day 2 — What happened, and when

Mini-lecture + timeline · unit word bank

Twelve minutes of direct teaching: Pearl Harbor (Dec 7 1941), Executive Order 9066 (Feb 19 1942), ten camps, 120,000 people, two out of three born in America, camps close 1945, apology and $20,000 in 1988. Students build a timeline strip. Then the unit word bank at the bottom of the hub — they write their own sentence for the first seven words.

MaterialsHub · word bank
Student productTimeline strip, 7 original sentences

Day 3 — Chapter 1, Parts 1–3

Vocabulary preview · read + guide

Vocabulary preview activity with self-check. Read Parts 1–3 aloud with the class on Easier; stronger readers switch to Harder and read independently. Stop after each part for the questions. Model question 4 (compete vs. cooperate) on the board.

Read + guide · past tense mini-lesson

Read Parts 4–6. The FBI checkbox question is a good partner task. Then the grammar box: regular ‑ed, irregular, and did not + base verb. Practice A and B with self-check.

Day 5 — Chapter 1 wrap

Quote study · drawing · paragraph

“Papa lost everything except his dignity.” Small groups list what Papa lost. Draw the boats turning around, label three things. Then the paragraph with frames. Print the page for students who write better on paper.

Day 6 — Chapter 2, Parts 1–2

Read + guide · pronoun mini-lesson

Read Parts 1–2. Question 7 (Jeanne is an outsider twice) is the heart of the chapter — give the Harder group time on it. Introduce subject vs. object pronouns with the we / they chart.

Day 7 — Chapter 2, Parts 3–4

Read + guide · close read of EO 9066

Read Parts 3–4. Project the actual text of Executive Order 9066 and find the words that never appear in it: Japanese, prison, camp. Discuss why. The two-teacher comparison table is a good dyad task.

Day 8 — Chapter 2, Parts 5–6

Read + guide · three-moves chart

Read Parts 5–6. The arrival scene rewards a second reading — the yellow swirl, the sound like rain. Fill the three-moves chart (Ocean Park / Terminal Island / Manzanar). That chart is essay evidence; tell them so.

Practice sections 1–4 · split dictation

Grammar practice page: past tense, negatives, pronouns, there was / there were. Students self-check and fix. Finish with a split dictation of the Manzanar arrival paragraph in pairs — A reads, B writes, then switch.

Thesis · evidence hunt

Give the essay prompt: what loss hurt the family most? Students go back through their two reading guides and the three-moves chart and pull quotations. Fill the planner on the assessment page — planner only, no drafting yet.

Model · write

Write one body paragraph together on the board using a student's thesis. Name the moves out loud: claim, evidence, this shows, so what. Then they write their own first body paragraph.

If you have extra days

Korematsu v. United StatesThe Supreme Court said the camps were legal in 1944. The Court finally repudiated that decision in 2018. Good one-day close read for the Harder group.
The 1988 apologyRead the letter President Bush sent to survivors. Compare to the anticipation guide statement about whether it could happen today.
Barefoot GenThe same war from the other side, in manga. Pairs well with the question of who is the enemy.
Later chaptersChapter 3–4 (the camp settles), Chapter 6 (Papa comes back), Chapter 12 (the loyalty oath), Chapter 22 (the return). Same template as Chapters 1 and 2.

One note before you start. Some of your students have been moved by a government too. Day 1 is designed so nobody has to disclose anything — they take positions on general statements, not on their own lives. Do not ask students to share their own migration stories in front of the class. If a student volunteers one in writing, answer it in writing.
