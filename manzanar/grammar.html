1. The simple past

Regularadd -ed: wave → waved, shout → shouted, arrest → arrested
-y → -iedcry → cried, carry → carried, worry → worried
Irregulargo→went, come→came, see→saw, know→knew, take→took, wear→wore, hold→held, give→gave, leave→left, build→built, feel→felt, sleep→slept
went · came · saw · knew · took · wore · held · gave · left · felt · slept · built
• Twenty-five boats (go) out to the ocean.
• The FBI (take) Papa away two weeks later.
• Papa (wear) rubber boots and a red sweater.
• Mama (cry) for many days.
• The family (leave) Terminal Island in February.
• Jeanne (sleep) in Mama's bed until Papa came back.
• The soldiers (build) the camp in the desert.
• Jeanne (feel) excited on the bus.
• The government (give) each family a number.
• Jeanne (see) her father one year later.

2. Negative past: did not + base verb

After did not, the verb goes back to its plain form.

Right: Papa did not fight.    Wrong: Papa did not fought.

• Papa did not (fought) the FBI men.
• Mama did not (knew) where they took him.
• The man did not (explained) what Pearl Harbor was.
• Jeanne did not (spoke) Japanese.
• The family did not (had) a choice.
• The teacher did not (helped) her in class.

3. Subject and object pronouns

Subject (before the verb)I · you · he · she · we · they
Object (after the verb, or after to / for / with / at)me · you · him · her · us · them

Chapter 2 is built on two words: we and they.

• (We / Us) moved three times in three months.
• The government moved (we / us) to the desert.
• (They / Them) closed Terminal Island in February.
• The family could not stop (they / them).
• Kiyo waited for (I / me) at the school door.
• (He / Him) found a safe way home.
• Mama worked at night, so we hardly saw (she / her).
• (She / Her) wanted to keep the family together.
• Papa made the FBI men walk behind (he / him).
• The soldiers gave (we / us) blankets and straw.

4. There was / there were

One thing → there was.   Two or more things → there were.

• twenty-five boats on the water that Sunday.
• one light bulb in each room.
• no explanation for the boats coming back.
• ten thousand people at Manzanar.
• a barbed wire fence around the whole camp.

5. Cause and effect: because and so

because + the reason  →  Papa burned the flag because he was afraid.

so + the result  →  Papa was afraid, so he burned the flag.

• Mama moved to Terminal Island she wanted to be near other Japanese families.
• The other kids spoke Japanese, Jeanne felt like an outsider.
• The Navy closed Terminal Island it was next to a naval base.
• The family had no home to return to, leaving the camp was frightening.

6. Combine with although and but

Use although to show a surprising contrast. Rewrite each pair as ONE sentence.

•. Papa lost his boat, his money, and his freedom. He kept his dignity.
•. Woody's family was imprisoned by the United States. Woody joined the U.S. Army.
